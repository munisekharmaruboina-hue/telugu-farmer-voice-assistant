CROP: Maize
TELUGU NAME: మొక్కజొన్న
CROP TYPE: Cereal / Field crop

GENERAL INFORMATION:
Maize is grown for grain, food, feed and other uses.
Crop performance depends on variety, season,
soil fertility, water and pest management.

COMMON CROP PROBLEMS:
1. Fall armyworm: feeding damage and irregular holes
   may appear on leaves, especially around the whorl.
2. Leaf blight: elongated lesions may develop on leaves.
3. Stalk rot: stems may become weak or decay.
4. Water stress: leaf rolling and poor growth may occur.
5. Nutrient problems: leaf colour and growth may change.

SAFE PRECAUTIONS:
- Inspect the whorl of young maize plants.
- Look for feeding damage and insect larvae.
- Monitor crop regularly, especially during early growth.
- Maintain suitable field drainage.
- Avoid unnecessary pesticide applications.
- Follow integrated pest management guidance.

INFORMATION TO COLLECT:
- Crop age and variety.
- Sowing date.
- Whether damage is in the whorl or on older leaves.
- Presence of larvae or insect droppings.
- Whether plants are drying or breaking.
- Recent rainfall and irrigation.

FERTILIZER SAFETY:
Nutrient requirements depend on soil test,
crop variety, season and irrigation conditions.

CROP CALENDAR - ANDHRA PRADESH:

Maize can be cultivated during Kharif and Rabi seasons.

KHARIF:
The ANGRAU package of practices gives June 15 to July 15
as the general Kharif sowing period.

RABI:
The ANGRAU package gives October 15 to November 15
for Rayalaseema districts.
For coastal Andhra Pradesh, it gives a period extending
up to the first week of January.

These are reference periods and not guaranteed dates
for every district, variety or farming condition.

Sowing time depends on:
- District and local climate.
- Onset and distribution of rainfall.
- Rainfed or irrigated cultivation.
- Soil moisture and drainage.
- Suitable variety and crop duration.

Do not give an exact sowing date without sufficient
local information.

Ask the farmer for their district and whether the crop
is rainfed or irrigated.
