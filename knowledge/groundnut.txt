CROP: Groundnut
TELUGU NAME: వేరుశెనగ
CROP TYPE: Oilseed / Legume

GENERAL INFORMATION:
Groundnut is an important oilseed crop in Andhra Pradesh.
Its growth is affected by soil conditions, rainfall,
irrigation, seed quality and crop management.

COMMON CROP PROBLEMS:
1. Early leaf spot: small brown spots may appear on leaves.
2. Late leaf spot: dark leaf spots may develop.
3. Rust: reddish-brown pustules may appear on leaves.
4. Stem or root rot: plants may wilt or dry.
5. Bud necrosis: young plant parts may show abnormal growth.

SAFE PRECAUTIONS:
- Inspect both upper and lower leaf surfaces.
- Monitor plants for leaf spots and rust-like symptoms.
- Avoid unnecessary waterlogging.
- Maintain field sanitation.
- Remove severely affected plant debris appropriately.
- Do not assume every yellow leaf is a nutrient deficiency.

INFORMATION TO COLLECT:
- Crop age.
- Variety.
- Rainfed or irrigated crop.
- Leaf spots, yellowing or wilting.
- Whether roots or stems are affected.
- Whether symptoms occur in patches or throughout the field.

FERTILIZER SAFETY:
Use soil-test-based nutrient recommendations
appropriate to the local soil and crop stage.

CROP CALENDAR - ANDHRA PRADESH:

Groundnut is mainly cultivated as a rainfed Kharif crop
in Andhra Pradesh.

The Andhra Pradesh State Seeds Development Corporation
mentions May to June, depending on monsoon rainfall.

ICAR-Indian Institute of Groundnut Research gives
June 15 to July 31 as the Kharif sowing period
and November to December for Rabi cultivation.

These are general reference periods, not fixed dates
for every district or farming condition.

The suitable sowing period depends on:
- District and local agro-climatic conditions.
- Onset and distribution of rainfall.
- Rainfed or irrigated cultivation.
- Soil moisture and field conditions.

Do not give an exact sowing date without sufficient
local information.

Ask the farmer for their district and whether the crop
is rainfed or irrigated.
